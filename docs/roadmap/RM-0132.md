# write-memory (pool): the daemon's memory grows under agent writes

Two soaks point at the same growth, and the owner's rule makes a leak a critical defect: a hub serves up to ten people in parallel for weeks.

**Evidence:**
- DL15 (RM-0127, `doc-library`): 0.96 MiB/min over 120 s with 8 MCP writers.
- GU16 (RM-0129, `specengine-guide`): 1.68 MiB/min over the second half with 8 writers (0.64-0.73 over the whole run); 0.0 with readers alone. The guide's reads take no turn and hold nothing, so the growth is the write path's.

**The task asks for:**
- a soak isolating the write path: the same 8 writers over `/mcp`, no readers, at least 5 minutes, two runs; the slope as Theil-Sen over the last two thirds, against a written budget, a breach failing the run;
- then the same with readers alone and with both, to confirm the split;
- the cause, named with its evidence (a cache, a session, a memo, an allocator plateau), and a targeted fix accepted by a clean re-run;
- if the growth is a plateau, not a leak, the run that shows it level.

Nothing blocks on it (ADR-0012); it is the next must after RM-0129.

Specified 2026-11-20 on the owner's word of that day ("fix what remains, to the end"): `docs/features/write-memory.md` (WM1-WM10: the write soak, its variants W, U, R, M and H, the cause, one fix, a clean re-run; WM10 a Linux owner's gate that blocks nothing). ADR-0086 records his stability policy (canon `docs/canon/soak-runs.md`, the rule line `docs/canon/architecture.md#stability`). Budget 0.25 MiB/min for writes; reads keep 0.5.

Diagnosed 2026-11-20 (`docs/features/write-memory.md` "Cause"):
- W keeps nothing: each write's corpus load is transient, and RSS's plateau is libmalloc keeping freed pages.
- The only growth is the check memo's D entries, 0.54 KiB per update, whose budget counts about 2.7 times less than they hold.
- The judge moved from the footprint to the quiet probe's live heap.

The fix: the memo counts its real bytes, and the root leaves D's key; `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=2` goes in the hub's image. WM10 stays the owner's gate.
