# hub-confirm (Phase 7, step 9): the UI confirms under a named login, with `[team]` and a person in `Author`

Zone leads confirm brief changes through the UI (`[zones]` with `[team]`), the owner setting global baselines; a step-up at the proxy guards confirm routes. Issues `hub-confirm`, which supersedes ADR-0035's terminal clause in hub mode only. No MCP tool or credential reaches a confirm route. `crates/specengine-http/tests/door.rs` is rewritten for the hub, whose local-only routes (`docs/canon/hub.md` "Routes") open here; local-mode fence tests pass unchanged; ADR-0054's forms stay stage-only and local. 4-6 pipeline hours, about an hour of it `[team]`, moved here from RM-0049 with its first reader.

- **`[team]`.** Per project, in `specengine.toml`, `[team.<login>]` with `name`, `role`, `zones`. `zones` name that file's `[zones]`, so `spec check` can judge them, and a role change is a reviewed commit (owner's decision 1); a host file would be a second truth outside git. The reader is a core change (`project_toml.rs` `RawFile` denies unknown keys, so the new table makes older binaries refuse the file).
- **Who confirms.** The login door says who enters, `[team]` who confirms. An identity not in `[team]` reads and makes the five plain writes, and confirms nothing. The record keeps the login; `name` is shown from here, never stored in its place, since a rename would break `by` and reviewer compares.
- **`Decided-by` and `Author`.** The two promises `hub-identity` could not keep without a confirm route: two identities give two `Decided-by` values, and `Author` names a person. Its AC-05 is taken here in full, M a default committer. A trailer needs an email: a fourth key or `<login>@<public host>`, settled here.
- **`by`.** A role in `by` is this step's call: on a hub `as` is a login, so `by = ["lead"]` refuses everyone until then, and a project lists logins.
- **Accept and clear take the login (criterion).** `accept_card`, `clear_card` and `clear_group` do not call `hub::acting`, and `CardAcceptBody` defaults to `local`, so adding them to the hub's write list alone would let any login accept as anyone. The step runs `acting` in each, and a test proves it: on a hub, a name other than the login on each of the three is a 403 and changes nothing, and a null `as` records the login. M: dropping `acting` from any of the three.
- **A suspect mark.** This step decides whether editing a target or cancelling may take a suspect mark away: today `edit --target -` or a cancel does, without `clear`'s consent, locally too (`docs/canon/hub.md` "Known limits").
